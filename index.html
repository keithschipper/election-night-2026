From election night, Decision Desk HQ margins fill in automatically every 30 minutes (6 PM ET Nov 3 to 6 PM ET Nov 6). Anything you type overrides the automatic value for that county; clear the box to go back to it. Type a margin as D+4.2, R+3, or a signed number (positive = Democrat ahead). Wait until most of a county is counted, and remember early votes and Election Day votes can lean very differently. Rows are in poll-closing order (Eastern time). Margin 2024 is the presidential result, Democrat minus Republican, in points.

How the projection works

  1. For each county you enter, the swing is its 2026 margin minus its 2024 presidential margin.
  2. Counties are grouped as suburban, working-class/swing, or Hispanic-heavy, and each group's swings are averaged. A group with nothing reported yet uses the average of all counties entered.
  3. Each race applies a weighted mix of those group swings to its 2024 presidential baseline. The weights are my judgment about each electorate (Michigan leans on suburban and working-class voters; Nevada more on Hispanic voters) and are listed on each card.
  4. The candidate adjustment slider adds anything the swing can't see, such as incumbency or a weak nominee. It starts at zero.
  5. Uncertainty combines a fixed 3-point error with the spread of the swings you've entered, shrinking as more counties report. The win chance and 80% range come from that.
  6. Once Decision Desk reports a race's own count, the projection blends that count with the model, giving the count more weight as the share of expected vote grows (weight = share1.5, so 50% in is about a third count, two-thirds model). The early count can mislead: mail and early votes are often counted first, and Nevada keeps counting mail ballots for days.

This is a back-of-the-envelope model, not a forecast. It assumes the national swing carries over evenly, which it often doesn't. 2024 county and district results are from Wikipedia's state result pages and Ballotpedia, linked in Sources.
